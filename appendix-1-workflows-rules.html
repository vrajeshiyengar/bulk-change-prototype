Appendix 1 · Workflows and rules

Every block, warning, approval and batch check in a bulk change comes from a rule. Rules are configured once, attached to the workflows they govern, versioned, and previewed before they go live. The same model serves every update to employee data, so a rule behaves the same whether one person changes or five thousand.

1The model in one picture

Configuration is set by rule owners; runtime objects are created by each change. A RuleEvaluation points at a RuleVersion, not at the rule, so the audit trail reads "flagged under band rules v4, set by Priya Shah on 1 Oct" even after the rule changes.

3How rules attach to workflows

Workflow ⟷ WorkflowRule is many-to-many, joined by a WorkflowRuleBinding.A workflow is an operation on an entity, such as "Employee · update fields" or "Candidate · offer letter". One rule can sit on several workflows. What differs per workflow lives on the binding, not on the rule: on or off, the stage it runs at, a severity override, and its order.
RuleEmployee · update fieldsCandidate · offer letterEmployee · international transfer
Above the band maximumBlocksWarning, overridden on the bindingBlocks
Raise above your limit (approval)Finance approvesFinance approvesFinance approves
New manager has a scheduled terminationBlocks; re-checked on the effective dateNot attachedBlocks
  • Bulk change is not its own workflow. It runs "Employee · update fields" once per row, plus that workflow's batch rules. That is why the rules are the same for one person or five thousand.
  • Rules built into Rippling attach themselves to every workflow for their entity (reporting loops, legal entity), so nobody can forget them.

6What an admin can change

The rule's owner can edit

  • Parameters (thresholds, limits)
  • Who it applies to (population)
  • The effect, and the override policy
  • Approver, quorum, response time, escalation
  • Title, message and fix steps
  • Owner, effective dates, status
  • Per workflow, on the binding: on or off, stage, severity

Nobody can edit

  • The logic of rules built into Rippling (only their message)
  • Entity and level once a rule is active: that would be a different rule
  • Past versions: an edit creates a new version
  • Free-form code: conditions come from templates and the shared condition builder
