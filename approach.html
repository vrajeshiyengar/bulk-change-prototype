Bulk change · product case study · Vrajesh Iyengar

1How I chose the approach

The user: a non-technical HR admin who does this a few times a year: a comp cycle, a reorg, an office move. Every edit touches someone's pay or access and is audited. Also involved: the Finance approver, and the IT owner of each connected app. Range: a dozen people at a 10-person company up to thousands at a 5,000-person one.

OptionProsCons for this user
CSV round-tripFamiliar from Excel; any scale; comp is planned offline anywayNo validation or impact preview until upload; error loops
In-product gridLive validation and a diff; one place to review; fine at 12 rowsHarder to build; has to hold thousands of rows
API / MCPAutomatableWrong persona; no human review step
CopilotFastest way to state intentNobody can approve pay they can't see row by row

The pick

One bulk-change workspace. Segments say who: saved dynamic rules or static lists of employee IDs, referenced or built right inside the change. One grid, which looks like the profile's Edit mode across many rows, holds every value. CSV is a way into that grid. The copilot drafts into it and never submits. An API can come later on the same engine.

Where I changed my mindIn my last round I rejected an in-product grid outright. I was wrong. Review needs a grid anyway, because nobody can approve 400 comp changes inside a CSV. So CSV became one way into the grid, not a separate path.

Segments are reusable objectsRecognition over recallA vetted group is referenced, not rebuilt from memory.
One grid, many doorsTesler's lawFilter, CSV and AI share one set of safety checks.
Prevent before commitNorman: forcing functionsPay already run can't be undone cleanly.
Manage by exceptionVon RestorffHundreds of rows are past what anyone can scan.
AI drafts, people commitAutomation biasAn auditable pay change needs a person who checked it.
Defaults fail safeDefault effectNo change, no email, no app sync before the effective date.

Deliberately not used: goal-gradient, endowed progress and social proof. They push motivation, and this admin isn't short of motivation. They're short of confidence.
