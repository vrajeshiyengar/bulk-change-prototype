Bulk change · product case study · Vrajesh Iyengar

1How I chose the approach

The user: a non-technical HR admin who does this a few times a year: a comp cycle, a reorg, an office move, a laptop refresh. Every edit touches someone's pay or access and is audited. Also involved: the Finance and People approvers, and the IT owner of each connected app. Range: a dozen people at a 10-person company up to thousands at a 5,000-person one, across more than one country and currency.

OptionProsCons for this user
CSV round-tripFamiliar from Excel; any scale; comp is planned offline anywayNo validation or impact preview until upload; error loops
In-product workspaceLive validation and a diff; one place to review; works at 12 rows and at 5,000Harder to build; has to hold thousands of rows and hundreds of fields
API / MCPAutomatableWrong persona; no human review step
CopilotFastest way to state intentNobody can approve pay they can't see row by row

The pick

One bulk-change workspace in seven steps: Details → Segments → Fields → Values → Review & confirm → Impact & approvals → Submit & track.

StepWhat it decidesThe core decision
SegmentsExactly whoReusable dynamic or static segments, one condition builder for everything, a live count with a labelled delta
FieldsWhat changesPicked from a searchable catalogue of every field (standard, custom, app and connector), with descriptions and when each was last used
ValuesTo whatOne Change values operation (field, operation, who) previews and then executes into a grid that looks like the profile's Edit mode. Cell edits, CSV and AI are other ways in. Everything is undoable and logged
Review & confirmIs it rightA deterministic change list is the record; an AI summary cites it line by line; every number opens the people behind it
Impact & approvalsWhat it sets offA flowchart of every system that re-verifies, the decisions only the admin can make, and approvals routed per row by thresholds
Where I changed my mindIn my last round I rejected an in-product grid outright. I was wrong. Review needs a grid anyway, because nobody can approve 400 comp changes inside a CSV. So CSV became one way into the grid, not a separate path. While building it, I also merged "set a value" and "apply a rule" into one operation. Both mean "these people, this field, this value", and two tools for one idea made people ask which one wins.

Principles every screen answers to

Rippling commits; apps catch upMental-model matchA Slack failure must never look like it undid a raise.
The grid is the profile, multipliedJakob's lawNothing new to learn for a task done once a year.
Segments are reusable objectsRecognition over recallA vetted group is referenced, not rebuilt from memory.
One operation, many doorsTesler's lawChange values, cell edits, CSV and AI share one set of checks.
No attribute is specialConsistencyRules, filters and grouping work on every field type.
Prevent before commitNorman: forcing functionsPay already run can't be undone cleanly.
Every flag says why and who owns itError recoveryThe reason, the fix, and the rule's owner, one click from the cell.
Show approval needs while editingFeedforwardWhich rows wait for Finance is known before submit, not after.
Every number opens its peopleTrust calibrationCounts are verified, not believed.
Manage by exceptionVon RestorffHundreds of rows are past what anyone can scan.
AI drafts, people commitAutomation biasAn auditable pay change needs a person who checked it.
Defaults fail safeDefault effectNo change, no email, no app sync before the effective date.

Deliberately not used: goal-gradient, endowed progress and social proof. They push motivation, and this admin isn't short of motivation. They're short of confidence.
